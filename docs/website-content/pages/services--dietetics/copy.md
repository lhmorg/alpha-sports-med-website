## explainer.heading

What happens in dietetics?

## explainer.paragraphs[0]

A dietitian uses nutrition knowledge to assess eating patterns and discuss food choices in the context of your health. An appointment can cover the meals you already eat, concerns about symptoms and changes you want to make. It is also a place to untangle advice that has become confusing.

## explainer.paragraphs[1]

Alexandra is an Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD). Dietitians Australia explains the <a href="https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/working-dietetics/standards-and-scope/role-accredited-practising-dietitian">APD role and professional standards</a>.

## explainer.paragraphs[2]

Advice should leave room for food you enjoy. The next step might be a small adjustment to meal timing or a discussion about getting enough food around activity. You can ask how a recommendation fits your circumstances.

## faqHeading

Dietetics: your questions

## faqs[0].question

Do I need a referral?

## faqs[0].answer

You can book a private dietetics appointment without a referral. Funded care can have different requirements. If you have a GP referral or care plan, ask reception to confirm eligibility and provider acceptance before booking.

## faqs[1].question

Is a dietitian the same as a nutritionist?

## faqs[1].answer

Dietitians have university training in nutrition and dietetics, including dietary care in a health setting. Nutritionist training can vary. Ask about the qualifications and scope of the person you are considering.

## faqs[2].question

Will I be given a strict meal plan?

## faqs[2].answer

Alexandra takes a non-diet approach. You can discuss flexible advice that fits your preferences and circumstances. Tell her if meal plans or past food rules have been unhelpful.

## faqs[3].question

Can I book if I am not an athlete?

## faqs[3].answer

Yes. Alpha offers adult nutrition support for everyday food and health concerns as well as questions about fuelling activity.

## faqs[4].question

Can I claim a rebate?

## faqs[4].answer

Rebates depend on your fund, cover and the provider or service. Medicare-funded care also has eligibility and referral requirements. Check with your insurer and reception before your appointment; a rebate is not guaranteed.

## faqs[5].question

What should I bring?

## faqs[5].answer

Bring relevant medical information or existing results if you have them, and any questions you would like to ask. A written food diary is not required to start the conversation.

## faqs[6].question

Can dietetics replace medical care?

## faqs[6].answer

Nutrition advice can sit alongside medical care. Keep your GP or treating team involved in symptoms or health conditions that need medical assessment.
