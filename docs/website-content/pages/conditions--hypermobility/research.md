## Sources and facts

Current Alpha Flex page describes individual assessment, strength/control rehabilitation, and collaboration; clinical leadership by Dr Ashton Wilson, osteopath. Its FAQ explicitly says clinic cannot formally diagnose hEDS/HSD, can assess movement and support referral to GP/specialist. It advertises Newport, Ascot Vale, Bacchus Marsh, Hawthorn and nationwide telehealth. This supports clinic service availability but not today's appointment slots. Claims that all participating clinicians completed particular courses need individual records before credential cards. No lived-experience autobiography was verified here.
H2 https://www.ehlers-danlos.com/diagnosis/ — current primary specialist-organisation source. Joint hypermobility can be symptomatic or asymptomatic; HSD involves symptomatic hypermobility after excluding other explanations. hEDS has clinical diagnostic criteria, no currently available genetic test; childhood framework differs. HSD is not simply a lesser degree of hEDS. Diagnosis requires history/examination and exclusion, not a single screening score.
H3 https://www.ehlers-danlos.com/physical-therapy/ — opened, current specialist clinical information. Individual strengthening/control and movement education can support EDS/HSD joint function. Avoid blanket stretching prohibition: careful stretching may be appropriate for genuinely tight muscles without stretching already hypermobile joints. Programme tolerance and coexisting conditions matter.
H4 https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng206/chapter/recommendations — official ME/CFS guideline; if hypermobility coexists with ME/CFS/post-exertional malaise, do not apply automatic progressive exercise or 'push through' instructions. See invisible-illnesses ledger for boundaries.
