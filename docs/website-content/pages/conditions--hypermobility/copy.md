## explainer.eyebrow

Understanding the terms

## explainer.heading

Hypermobility, HSD and hEDS

## explainer.paragraphs[0]

Joint hypermobility describes a greater range of movement at one or more joints. Hypermobility spectrum disorders (HSD) involve symptomatic hypermobility after other explanations are considered. Hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (hEDS) has clinical diagnostic criteria.

## explainer.paragraphs[1]

HSD is not simply a milder version of hEDS. Either can affect daily life and need support. Diagnosis considers history, examination and other possible explanations; it cannot be made from flexibility or a checklist alone.

## explainer.paragraphs[2]

Alpha can assess movement and provide musculoskeletal support. We do not formally diagnose hEDS or HSD. Discuss diagnostic questions with your GP or specialist; appropriate referral can be considered.

## faqHeading

Hypermobility: your questions

## faqs[0].question

Does being flexible mean I have hEDS?

## faqs[0].answer

No. Joint hypermobility can occur without a disorder. hEDS has clinical diagnostic criteria and requires assessment of more than flexibility. Discuss diagnostic concerns with your GP or specialist.

## faqs[1].question

Can Alpha diagnose HSD or hEDS?

## faqs[1].answer

No. Alpha can assess movement and offer musculoskeletal support, but does not formally diagnose hEDS or HSD. Your GP or specialist can help you seek appropriate diagnostic assessment.

## faqs[2].question

Is HSD a milder form of hEDS?

## faqs[2].answer

No. They are distinct diagnostic categories. The name alone does not tell you how much someone’s symptoms affect daily life.

## faqs[3].question

Should I avoid all stretching?

## faqs[3].answer

Not necessarily. Some tight muscles may benefit from careful stretching, and already mobile joints may need other approaches. Ask for advice that considers the joint, symptoms and your individual circumstances.

## faqs[4].question

Should I keep increasing my exercise?

## faqs[4].answer

Exercise needs to be considered around your tolerance and other health conditions. Do not assume fixed increases suit you. If you have ME/CFS or worsening after exertion, seek medical or specialist guidance rather than pushing through.

## faqs[5].question

## faqs[5].answer

Alpha offers telehealth for hypermobility support. Contact reception to confirm current availability, suitability and what the appointment can cover.
