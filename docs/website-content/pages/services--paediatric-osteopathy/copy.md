## explainer.eyebrow

The scope of osteopathy

## explainer.heading

Understanding paediatric osteopathy

## explainer.paragraphs[0]

Osteopathy focuses on musculoskeletal concerns, such as pain and how the body moves. A consultation considers your child’s history and whether the concern is within that scope. A symptom or head-turning preference does not establish a diagnosis on its own.

## explainer.paragraphs[1]

We do not claim osteopathy treats colic, reflux, feeding or sleep difficulties, or developmental conditions. Those concerns need appropriate medical or child-health advice. A discussion about movement must not delay that care.

## explainer.paragraphs[2]

<a href="https://osteopathy.org.au/Web/Advocacy/Position-Statement-pages/Osteopathic-Clinical-Management-for-Babies-Infants-and-Children.aspx">Osteopathy Australia’s position on care for babies and children</a> recommends against spinal manipulative techniques for children under 12. It also sets out the need for age-appropriate assessment, consent and referral precautions.

## explainer.paragraphs[3]

Ask the osteopath to explain any proposed approach, its evidence and risks before you decide. Describing a technique as gentle does not establish that it is suitable, effective or risk-free.

## faqHeading

Paediatric osteopathy: your questions

## faqs[0].question

Does osteopathy treat colic or reflux?

## faqs[0].answer

We do not claim osteopathy treats colic or reflux. Feeding, sleep and digestive concerns should be discussed with your GP, paediatrician or maternal and child health nurse. Osteopathy must not delay that advice.

## faqs[1].question

Is osteopathy safe for every baby?

## faqs[1].answer

No treatment can be described as automatically suitable or risk-free for every baby. The concern, age, health history and proposed approach need consideration. Ask about the evidence, risks and alternatives before consenting.

## faqs[2].question

Will my child have spinal manipulation?

## faqs[2].answer

Osteopathy Australia recommends against spinal manipulative techniques in babies and children under 12. Ask the osteopath what they propose for your child and how it aligns with that guidance before agreeing to care.

## faqs[3].question

How young can a child be seen?

## faqs[3].answer

Contact reception with your child’s age and the reason for your enquiry. Reception can help you check whether an appointment is appropriate.

## faqs[4].question

Can I ask questions before agreeing to treatment?

## faqs[4].answer

Yes. Ask the osteopath to explain the assessment or proposed care, its risks and alternatives. A parent or authorised carer is involved in consent when a child cannot provide it.

## faqs[5].question

How many appointments will my child need?

## faqs[5].answer

That depends on the assessment and whether osteopathic care is appropriate. Ask what would be reviewed and why another appointment is recommended.

## faqs[6].question

Can I claim private health insurance?

## faqs[6].answer

Eligibility depends on your cover, the practitioner and the service. Confirm fees and any funding requirements with reception and your insurer before attending.
