Understanding the service

## What does an exercise physiologist do?

Exercise physiologists assess your exercise needs and prescribe a programme for your health and activity goals. They coach you through movements and review the exercises as your needs change. Relevant movement or functional tasks may form part of the assessment.

In Australia, Exercise & Sport Science Australia (ESSA) accredits exercise physiologists. Qualification pathways include an accredited undergraduate degree or exercise science study followed by postgraduate clinical training.

An exercise physiologist focuses on clinical exercise planning and supervision. A <a href="/services/physiotherapy/">physiotherapist</a> assesses and treats movement problems using exercise and other approaches, including hands-on techniques where appropriate. Personal trainers generally work with fitness and training goals; their qualifications and experience vary.

The roles overlap, so the appointment you choose depends on the guidance you need and your health history. Reception can help you choose. Practitioners may work together where that suits your care.

How we work

## From assessment to an exercise plan

Your exercise physiologist explains why particular exercises have been chosen and discusses how to fit them into your week.

### Tell us where you're starting
We discuss your health and activity history, including what you're doing now and what has been difficult.

### Assess what's relevant
Your exercise physiologist chooses movement or functional checks that are relevant to your needs.

### Agree on practical goals
We discuss what you want to work towards and how your current health and activity affect that goal.

### Choose suitable exercises
Your exercise physiologist selects exercises for your starting point and explains how much to do and when to adjust them.

### Practise with guidance
Work through the movements with your exercise physiologist. Ask questions about technique or anything that feels unfamiliar.

### Review the plan
Discuss how you've responded, what you've managed between appointments and what needs to change.

## Your first exercise physiology appointment

Bring your questions. You don't need to arrive with an exercise programme or a clear idea of which exercises to choose.

### Talk through your history
Discuss your health, current activity and goals. Let us know about relevant medical advice and anything you're worried about.

### Look at movement and function
Your exercise physiologist may check movement or function to understand your starting point. The checks depend on what you have come in for.

### Discuss an initial approach
We explain the assessment in plain language and agree on suitable exercises with you, including how they fit around your current care.

### Plan practice and review
Agree on what to practise between appointments and when to review it. Your exercise physiologist explains how to monitor your response.

### Wear comfortable clothing
Choose clothes and shoes that let you move comfortably.

### Bring relevant information
Bring any referral, medication information or medical advice relevant to exercise, if you have it.

Exercise physiology FAQs

## How is exercise physiology different from physiotherapy?
Exercise physiology centres on clinical exercise assessment, planning and review. Physiotherapists assess and treat movement problems using exercise and other approaches, which may include hands-on techniques. The roles overlap. Your needs help determine which appointment is suitable, and reception can help you choose.

## How is an exercise physiologist different from a personal trainer?
An exercise physiologist has university training in clinical exercise for health, injury and activity needs. Personal trainers generally help with fitness and training goals; their qualifications and experience vary. If a health condition or injury affects your exercise choices, discuss what support you need before starting.

## Do I need a referral, and can I claim a rebate?
Ask reception whether you need a referral for your appointment and whether Jordan accepts the funding arrangement you plan to use. Exercise physiology is eligible for Medicare subsidies under GP chronic condition management arrangements when you have the required plan and referral. The limited sessions are shared with other allied health services. Confirm your eligibility and the fees before booking. For private health benefits, check your policy with your insurer and the provider details with reception.

## Can I book if I'm new to exercise?
Yes. You don't need a sporting background. Tell your exercise physiologist what you've done before and what you'd like to start doing. The assessment and initial programme take your starting point into account.

## Can I exercise if I have pain or a health condition?
Your symptoms and health history guide whether an exercise approach is appropriate. Tell your exercise physiologist about changes in your symptoms, relevant medical advice and any restrictions you've been given. Exercise advice can sit alongside care from your GP or other treating practitioners.

## What should I bring to my appointment?
Wear clothes and shoes you can move in. Bring relevant referrals or medical information if available, plus any questions about exercise. A rough idea of your usual activity and what you'd like to work towards is useful.
