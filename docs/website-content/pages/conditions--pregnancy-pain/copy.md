## urgent.heading

Get medical advice for warning symptoms

## urgent.paragraphs[0]

Contact your maternity team immediately about vaginal bleeding, fluid loss, reduced or changed baby movements, severe headache or vision changes, or severe abdominal pain. Do not book a musculoskeletal appointment first for these symptoms.

## urgent.paragraphs[1]

If symptoms are severe or life-threatening, call 000. If you are unsure about a new symptom in pregnancy, contact your midwife, doctor or maternity service for advice.

## explainer.eyebrow

Understanding the scope

## explainer.heading

Back and pelvic pain during pregnancy

## explainer.paragraphs[0]

Pregnancy-related pelvic girdle pain concerns the joints around the pelvis and can affect everyday movement. Back pain can also arise during pregnancy. An assessment considers the symptoms and whether they fit a musculoskeletal concern.

## explainer.paragraphs[1]

Pain at the front of the pelvis is sometimes called pubic symphysis pain. A description of pain does not tell you its cause; new or worrying symptoms need medical advice rather than online reassurance.

## explainer.paragraphs[2]

The Royal Women’s Hospital provides information about pregnancy-related pelvic girdle pain and practical support. Individual advice should take your pregnancy and medical care into account.

## faqHeading

Pregnancy-related pain: your questions

## faqs[0].question

What kind of pregnancy pain can I ask about?

## faqs[0].answer

You can ask about musculoskeletal concerns, such as back or pelvic pain affecting movement. It does not cover diagnosis or treatment of obstetric complications. New or worrying pregnancy symptoms need advice from your maternity team.

## faqs[1].question

Is treatment automatically safe during pregnancy?

## faqs[1].answer

No approach is automatically appropriate for every pregnancy. Your health history, pregnancy circumstances and proposed care need consideration. Ask about the options, evidence and risks, and share any maternity-team restrictions.

## faqs[2].question

Do you provide internal pelvic-floor assessments?

## faqs[2].answer

For an internal pelvic-floor assessment, ask your GP or maternity team about an appropriately trained pelvic-health provider. The appointments described here focus on musculoskeletal concerns.

## faqs[3].question

Can I book in the first trimester?

## faqs[3].answer

Tell reception how many weeks pregnant you are and why you are enquiring. Suitability and the available practitioner need to be confirmed. Bleeding or abdominal pain in early pregnancy needs medical advice first.

## faqs[4].question

Do I need a referral?

## faqs[4].answer

You can enquire about a private appointment without a referral. Tell the clinician who provides your maternity care and bring relevant advice. Funded care can have separate requirements.

## faqs[5].question

Can I keep exercising?

## faqs[5].answer

That depends on your pregnancy and individual circumstances. Follow maternity-team advice and restrictions. A clinician can discuss suitable musculoskeletal exercise support within those limits.

## faqs[6].question

How many appointments will I need?

## faqs[6].answer

That depends on your assessment and response. Discuss a review point and what would make further care useful.
