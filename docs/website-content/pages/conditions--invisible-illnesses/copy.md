## explainer.eyebrow

A description, not a diagnosis

## explainer.heading

What we mean by invisible illnesses

## explainer.paragraphs[0]

Invisible illness is a broad description for health problems that may not be apparent to other people. It is not one diagnosis, and there is no single exercise or treatment approach that fits everyone included under the term.

## explainer.paragraphs[1]

Alpha’s role is musculoskeletal assessment and support when appropriate. Diagnosis and treatment of underlying conditions such as ME/CFS, POTS, autoimmune conditions or endometriosis remain with your medical team.

## explainer.paragraphs[2]

If you have a specific pain or movement concern, we can discuss whether it falls within our scope alongside your existing medical care. A clinic appointment should not replace investigation or advice from your GP or specialist.

## note.eyebrow

ME/CFS and post-exertional symptoms

## note.heading

General exercise advice may be inappropriate

## note.paragraphs[0]

For people with ME/CFS, symptoms can worsen after exertion. General exercise advice may make symptoms worse, and exercise is not a cure for ME/CFS. Fixed increases in activity or advice to push through symptoms are inappropriate.

## note.paragraphs[1]

The NICE ME/CFS guideline recommends specialist-team oversight for any individual exercise programme that a person chooses to undertake. Musculoskeletal support at Alpha must sit alongside that specialist advice.

## note.paragraphs[2]

If you have ME/CFS or delayed symptom worsening after activity, discuss this with your medical or specialist team before considering exercise advice. This concern must not be treated as ordinary lack of fitness.

## faqHeading

Invisible illnesses: your questions

## faqs[0].question

Is invisible illness a diagnosis?

## faqs[0].answer

No. It describes health problems that may not be outwardly apparent. Different illnesses have different assessment and care needs; unexplained symptoms should be discussed with an appropriate medical professional.

## faqs[1].question

Does Alpha diagnose the underlying illness?

## faqs[1].answer

Alpha’s role here is musculoskeletal assessment and support. Diagnosis of ME/CFS, POTS, fibromyalgia, autoimmune conditions or other underlying illnesses requires appropriate medical assessment. Discuss diagnosis and medical treatment with your GP or specialist.

## faqs[2].question

Can I enquire while seeing another medical team?

## faqs[2].answer

Yes. Explain the specific concern, your current medical care and any restrictions. We can discuss whether an appropriate musculoskeletal appointment is available. Do not replace existing medical advice with a general exercise plan.

## faqs[3].question

What if activity makes my symptoms worse later?

## faqs[3].answer

Tell your medical or specialist team and any clinician you consult. Delayed worsening after exertion needs particular consideration. For ME/CFS, general exercise advice may worsen symptoms and exercise programmes require specialist oversight; fixed increases or pushing through are inappropriate.

## faqs[4].question

What information might be useful?

## faqs[4].answer

Relevant letters, diagnoses, restrictions or advice from your treating team may help the discussion. Explain how activity affects you and what you want assessed. You do not need to prove how ill you feel with a particular test result.

## faqs[5].question

Can you accommodate my appointment needs?

## faqs[5].answer

Contact reception to discuss what you need, including appointment format or access requirements. Check which arrangements are available before booking.
