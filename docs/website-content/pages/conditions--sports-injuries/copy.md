## urgent.eyebrow

Medical assessment comes first

## urgent.heading

Get help for a significant injury

## urgent.paragraphs[0]

Seek medical assessment for severe pain, a visible deformity or an inability to bear weight after an injury. A suspected fracture or dislocation needs appropriate medical care. If an injured limb is numb, pale or cold, call 000.

## urgent.paragraphs[1]

After a head knock or suspected concussion, stop sport and seek medical assessment. Do not return to play that day. Alpha’s musculoskeletal appointments do not replace emergency or concussion care.

## explainer.eyebrow

Understanding injury terms

## explainer.heading

Sprains, strains and other sports injuries

## explainer.paragraphs[0]

A sprain involves a ligament. A strain involves muscle or tendon. These terms cover different degrees of injury; the name alone does not tell you the appropriate treatment or return date.

## explainer.paragraphs[1]

Assessment considers your symptoms, injury history and what your activity asks of you. The clinician can discuss care within their scope and whether medical assessment or further investigation is needed.

## explainer.paragraphs[2]

Rest or activity modification can be necessary. Rehabilitation advice should account for the injury and your circumstances, rather than a blanket instruction to keep training.

## faqHeading

Sports injuries: your questions

## faqs[0].question

Should I book Alpha after a major injury?

## faqs[0].answer

Significant injuries may need medical care first. Seek medical assessment for severe pain, deformity, inability to bear weight or a suspected fracture or dislocation. An injured limb that is numb, pale or cold needs an ambulance on 000.

## faqs[1].question

What should I do after a head knock?

## faqs[1].answer

Stop sport and seek medical assessment for a head knock or suspected concussion. Do not return to play that day. Follow medical guidance; concussion assessment and return-to-play clearance need appropriate medical care.

## faqs[2].question

What is the difference between a sprain and a strain?

## faqs[2].answer

A sprain involves a ligament. A strain involves muscle or tendon. Both vary in severity and need assessment appropriate to the symptoms and injury.

## faqs[3].question

Do I need to stop all training?

## faqs[3].answer

That depends on the injury and your medical advice. Activity may need changing or stopping. Ask the treating clinician which activities are appropriate rather than assuming you should train through symptoms.

## faqs[4].question

When can I return to sport?

## faqs[4].answer

There is no universal timeline. The injury, your response and the demands of the sport matter, and some situations require medical clearance. Discuss readiness with your treating team.

## faqs[5].question

Will I need a scan?

## faqs[5].answer

Not every injury needs imaging. The appropriate clinician can consider your symptoms and whether investigation or medical assessment is indicated.

## faqs[6].question

Do I need a referral?

## faqs[6].answer

You can enquire about a private musculoskeletal appointment without a referral. Funding arrangements can have separate requirements. Confirm the service, fee and any eligibility conditions before attending.
